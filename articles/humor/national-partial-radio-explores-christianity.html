National Partial Radio Explores Christianity by Interviewing Two People Who Agree With National Partial Radio

A fearless investigation into the full range of Christian thought, provided nobody wanders too far outside the approved guest list.

RH
Reality Herald Humor DeskSeptember 8, 2026 · 4 min read
Filed underHumor & Satire
Article typeSATIRE

National Partial Radio announced Monday that a months-long investigation had finally uncovered the full range of Christian belief after producers located two clergy members who agreed with one another, agreed with the producers, and could both join the same afternoon recording slot.

“We wanted listeners to hear Christianity in all of its complexity,” explained senior producer Avery Balance, who described the booking process as exhaustive. “We considered interviewing Christians who hold historic Christian teachings, but that would have introduced a perspective our audience might mistake for Christianity.”

The resulting segment, titled Faith in Transition: What Christians Really Think Now, opened with a reminder that Christianity contains many traditions. The program then explored those traditions by speaking with two pastors whose answers differed mainly in sentence length.

One guest explained that traditional Christian teaching should undergo continuous revision whenever tension arises between inherited doctrine and current cultural preferences. The second guest agreed, while adding that inherited doctrine can sometimes remain useful as background scenery.

A representative sample

Producers emphasized that the two-person sample captured the breadth of a global religion with remarkable efficiency. Researchers had initially proposed interviewing Catholics, Orthodox Christians, Lutherans, Baptists, Pentecostals, Anglicans, Reformed Christians, Anabaptists, African churches, Asian churches, Latin American churches, and Christians who could quote a creed written before 1965.

That plan was rejected after a staff meeting concluded that additional guests might complicate the segment.

“Journalism requires choices,” Balance said. “Some voices illuminate a subject. Other voices create the dangerous impression that people still believe what their churches formally teach.”

All views represented

National Partial Radio later defended the segment under a new editorial initiative called All Views Represented. Under the policy, producers promise to represent every major viewpoint that survives the guest-selection process.

Upcoming installments will examine American conservatism through two former Republicans who now vote Democratic, atheism through two Episcopal priests, and homeschooling through a district superintendent and a teachers-union lobbyist.

Asked whether future religion coverage might include Christians who defend historic Christian moral teaching, Balance said producers remain open to the possibility.

“Absolutely,” Balance replied. “We welcome every perspective. We would simply need to find someone who can explain why that perspective should no longer guide anyone.”

Satire: This article describes a fictional broadcaster and fictional people. The piece parodies selective framing in media coverage and should not be read as a factual news report.
