TEACHER SECTION

Union Propaganda

A developing source-analysis section examining claims made by teachers' unions and education advocacy organizations. The purpose is not to assume that union claims are false, but to separate evidence, advocacy, bargaining interests, political messaging, and empirical claims so teachers can evaluate each on its merits.

Claim vs. evidence

Identify what an organization claims and what evidence actually supports the claim.

Who benefits?

Distinguish arguments about students, teachers, institutions, and bargaining power.

Funding claims

Compare rhetoric about education spending with budgets, enrollment, salaries, benefits, and outcomes.

Political messaging

Separate professional representation from political advocacy and campaign language.

Counter-sources

Compare union claims with government data, academic studies, school districts, and competing organizations.

Rhetorical framing

Watch for emotionally loaded terms that may conceal disputed premises.
