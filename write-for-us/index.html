CONTRIBUTORS

Write for Reality Herald

Reality Herald welcomes serious writers who can contribute reporting, analysis, argument, historical work, theological reflection, criticism, and scholarship with clarity, evidence, and intellectual fairness.

What We Are Looking For

Reality Herald is intended to develop as a multi-author publication rather than a personal blog. We are interested in writers who take evidence, argument, history, and competing claims seriously.

Contributors do not need to agree with one another. Strong submissions should make their reasoning visible, distinguish fact from interpretation, acknowledge meaningful objections, and avoid treating assertion as proof.

Article Formats

REPORT

Reporting

Original reporting, interviews, document-based stories, and explanatory journalism.

Typical length: 800–1,800 words
ANALYSIS

Analysis

Careful interpretation of events, ideas, institutions, research, or historical developments.

Typical length: 1,000–2,500 words
ARGUMENT

Essays & Arguments

Defended positions in politics, culture, philosophy, theology, history, education, law, or related fields.

Typical length: 1,200–3,000 words
HISTORY & THEOLOGY

Historical & Theological Work

Church history, historical theology, doctrine, councils, creeds, primary sources, biography, and Christianity in culture.

Typical length: 1,500–3,500 words
SCHOLARSHIP

Academic Commentary

Research notes, journal discussions, scholarly essays, literature reviews, and accessible treatments of academic work.

Typical length: 1,500–4,000 words
REVIEW

Reviews & Responses

Book reviews, review essays, responses, rejoinders, and thoughtful criticism of published arguments.

Typical length: 800–2,500 words

Editorial Standards

  • Originality: Submissions should normally consist of original work not previously published elsewhere.
  • Sourcing: Factual claims should rest on reliable sources. Authors should provide links, citations, or source notes where appropriate.
  • Distinctions: Reporting, analysis, argument, review, fact-checking, and satire should remain clearly identified.
  • Fairness: Writers should represent serious opposing views accurately before criticizing them.
  • Disclosure: Authors should disclose relevant financial, professional, organizational, or personal conflicts of interest.
  • Attribution: Quotations, ideas, data, images, and research drawn from others require appropriate attribution.
  • Corrections: Authors should cooperate promptly in correcting material factual errors.
SUBMISSIONS

How to Submit

For the prototype stage, Reality Herald is not yet accepting submissions through an automated form. The eventual submission process will request:

  • the proposed article title;
  • a short summary or pitch;
  • the complete manuscript or a writing sample;
  • a brief author biography;
  • relevant source notes or citations; and
  • disclosure of any relevant conflicts of interest.

A submission email address or form will be added before outside submissions formally open.

Peer-Reviewed Scholarly Articles

Reality Herald may publish a distinct class of scholarly articles under a formal double-anonymous peer-review process using two independent reviewers. The initial focus will include Christian legal thought and scholarly responses to legal arguments or legal scholars.

The Peer Reviewed designation will apply only to manuscripts that complete that process. Ordinary essays, commentary, reporting, and reviews will not carry that designation.

Editing, Rights, and Publication

Reality Herald expects to edit accepted work for clarity, accuracy, style, length, and organization in consultation with the author. Substantive changes should not silently alter an author's argument.

Final contributor terms—including copyright, republication rights, exclusivity periods, and compensation where applicable—will be stated clearly before formal submissions open.

Who Should Consider Contributing?

Journalists, scholars, historians, theologians, scientists, teachers, lawyers, clergy, graduate students, independent researchers, critics, and other serious writers are all potential contributors. Academic credentials can matter for some subjects, but clear reasoning, responsible research, and strong writing matter more than title alone.
