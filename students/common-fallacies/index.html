STUDENT SECTION

Common Fallacies

A practical guide to recurring errors in reasoning, with emphasis on how fallacies actually appear in debates rather than merely memorizing Latin names.

Straw man

Replacing an opponent's actual position with an easier position to attack.

Ad hominem

Substituting an attack on the person for an answer to the argument.

False dilemma

Presenting two choices when additional alternatives remain available.

Equivocation

Changing the meaning of a key term during the argument.

Begging the question

Smuggling the disputed conclusion into one of the premises.

Appeal to authority

Treating expertise as a substitute for evidence when the authority lacks relevant competence or consensus.
